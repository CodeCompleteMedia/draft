# Product

## Register

product

## Users

- **The teacher (Matt)** runs the draft from the admin board on a laptop, usually while the class watches the projector. Every action happens in front of about 30 high school students, so it has to be one drag or one click, easy to undo, and impossible to fumble.
- **The current captain** is a high school student standing at a shared Chromebook with the class behind them. They choose one teammate from anonymous trait cards, usually within a minute or two, then walk back to their seat.
- **The rest of the class** watches the projector from their seats in a lit classroom, waiting to see if their name is called.

## Product Purpose

Team Draft Day forms project teams as a sports-style draft. Students' personality test, e-skills, and personality form results come from a unified Google Sheet. A wheel of names picks captains. Captains pick teammates blind, judging traits instead of friendships. Each pick is revealed to the class with broadcast-style suspense.

Success means:
- a whole period's teams are formed within one class
- captains choose based on skills and personality
- the waiting students stay engaged instead of anxious
- nobody can work out the name behind a card

## Brand Personality

Sports draft night, as in NFL Draft broadcast energy: bold, suspenseful, and fair.

- **Class view** (the show): builds anticipation with the podium moment, the pause before a name, the payoff.
- **Captain station:** calm focus. The room is loud, and the screen shouldn't add to it.
- **Admin board:** control and speed.

The class view is the one surface allowed to be theatrical. The admin board and captain station follow the product register.

## Anti-references

Matt named none. The product itself implies one: nothing on the captain station may suggest who a card belongs to. That rules out avatars, per-person colors, initials, and ordering that follows the roster.

## Design Principles

1. **The pick is about traits, not people.** The captain station shows skills and personality, never identity. Layout, order, and styling carry no hints.
2. **Save the drama for the moment.** The class view goes big for spins and reveals, and stays a clean, readable board the rest of the time.
3. **The teacher is in control in front of an audience.** Every admin action is one gesture, visibly confirmed, and reversible.
4. **Whose turn it is is always obvious.** Every screen makes the current captain and the next step clear at a glance.
5. **Readable from the back row.** Anything the class needs to see works on a washed-out projector in a lit room.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **Projector:** names, team board, and turn banner must be legible from the back of a lit classroom. That means large type, heavy weights, and high contrast that survives a projector's washed-out blacks and whites.
- **Captain station:** a Chromebook at about 1366×768. Cards must scan quickly while standing.
- **Baseline:** WCAG 2.1 AA contrast. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` by shortening the spin and dropping the confetti.
